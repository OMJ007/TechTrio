## Economic moats

A moat is a durable structural advantage that lets a business keep earning
above-average returns while competitors try to take them away. There are only a
few genuine kinds: a brand people pay more for out of habit or trust; a cost
advantage rivals cannot replicate; network effects where each user makes the
product better for the next; switching costs that make leaving painful; and
regulatory or licensing protection.

Most things called moats are not. A good product is not a moat — it invites
imitation. A first-mover position is not a moat. High current growth is not a
moat. The test is simple: if a competitor with unlimited capital entered
tomorrow, what stops them?

## Circle of competence

Invest only in businesses you can actually explain — how they make money, what
would break them, who their customers are and why they stay. The circle does
not need to be large. Knowing where its edge lies matters far more than how
much it encloses.

The honest answer to "do I understand this?" is often no, and the correct
response to that is to pass, not to buy a smaller quantity.

## Margin of safety

Estimate what a business is worth, then pay meaningfully less. The gap is not
greed — it is protection against your own estimate being wrong, which it
frequently will be. A bridge engineered for 30 tonnes carries 10-tonne trucks.

This applies to an investor's confidence as much as to the price. The less
certain the estimate, the wider the discount required.

## Compounding

Money compounds; so do mistakes. Starting early beats starting large, and by a
margin most people find hard to credit until they run the arithmetic.

₹10,000 a month at 12% for 30 years becomes roughly ₹3.5 crore. Delay the start
by ten years and the same monthly amount produces about ₹1 crore. The ten years
cost more than the twenty that follow them earn.

The corollary is that costs compound too. A 1.5% expense ratio versus 0.2% on
an index fund does not sound like much and consumes a substantial share of a
lifetime's returns.

## Time horizon

The favourite holding period is forever. This is not sentimentality: trading
generates taxes, costs, and opportunities to be wrong twice instead of once.
Market volatility is the price of admission for equity returns, not a signal to
act on.

Be fearful when others are greedy and greedy when others are fearful. In
practice that means having cash and conviction available precisely when
everything looks worst, which is the hardest part of the discipline.

## For most people, index funds

The candid position: most investors, professional ones included, do not beat a
low-cost index fund over long periods. A regular investment into a broad index
fund, left alone for decades, beats the large majority of managed alternatives
after costs.

Stock picking is worth doing only if you genuinely enjoy the analysis, have the
temperament for being wrong in public, and can afford the outcome. It is a
choice, not a requirement.

## What to avoid

Speculation dressed as investing — anything whose return depends entirely on
someone paying more later rather than on the underlying producing cash.
Leverage, which turns a survivable mistake into a fatal one. And complexity
sold as sophistication: if a product needs a chart to explain its fee
structure, the fee structure is the product.
