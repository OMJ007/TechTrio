## What it is

Section 80C of the 1961 Act — **Section 123** under the Income-tax Act, 2025 —
allows a deduction of up to **₹1,50,000 per financial year** from taxable
income. The limit is a combined ceiling across every eligible instrument, not
per instrument. The eligible list now sits in Schedule XV of the new Act.

**This deduction is available only under the old regime.** If you are on the
new regime, 80C investments give you no tax benefit at all — which does not
make them bad investments, only tax-neutral ones.

At the 30% slab, a full ₹1.5 lakh claim saves ₹46,800 including cess. At 20%,
₹31,200. At 5%, ₹7,800.

## What counts

**Investments**

| Instrument | Lock-in | Return profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ELSS mutual funds | 3 years | Market-linked equity |
| PPF | 15 years, partial withdrawal from year 7 | ~7.1%, government-set |
| EPF (employee share) | Until retirement / exit | ~8.25%, government-set |
| NSC | 5 years | ~7.7%, fixed |
| Tax-saving fixed deposit | 5 years | 6–7.5%, fully taxable interest |
| Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana | Until daughter turns 21 | ~8.2%, girl child only |
| Senior Citizens Savings Scheme | 5 years | ~8.2%, age 60+ |
| ULIP | 5 years | Market-linked, high cost |

**Expenses that also qualify**

- Life insurance premiums for self, spouse, children
- Home loan **principal** repayment (the interest goes under 24(b) instead)
- Tuition fees for up to two children, at any Indian school, college or university
- Stamp duty and registration charges on a house purchase, in the year paid

## Choosing between them

ELSS has the shortest lock-in of any 80C instrument at three years and is the
only one with real equity upside. It is also the only one that can lose money in
the short run. For someone under 40 with a long horizon, it is usually the
better use of the limit.

PPF is the opposite trade: a 15-year commitment for a modest, guaranteed,
entirely tax-free return. It suits the debt portion of a portfolio and anyone
who wants certainty.

Tax-saving FDs are the weakest of the set — a five-year lock-in for a return
lower than PPF, and the interest is fully taxable on top. They exist mainly
because they are easy to buy at a bank counter.

ULIPs mix insurance and investment and charge for both. Buy term insurance and
invest separately instead.

## The order most people should fill it

1. **Count what is already there.** EPF deducted from salary, life insurance
   premiums, home loan principal, and children's tuition fees all count
   automatically. Many salaried people are already halfway to the limit before
   investing a rupee.
2. **Fill the gap with ELSS** if your horizon is five years or more.
3. **Use PPF** for the portion you want guaranteed, or if equity volatility
   would push you to sell at the wrong moment.
4. **Stop at ₹1.5 lakh.** There is no benefit beyond it under this section.

## Common mistakes

- Investing the full ₹1.5 lakh fresh without counting EPF, which is already
  being deducted — over-investing in a lock-in for no extra deduction.
- Buying an endowment or ULIP policy in March under time pressure, then paying
  premiums for years on a product that suits nobody.
- Claiming 80C while filing under the new regime. It is simply disallowed.
- Forgetting that the ELSS three-year lock-in applies to **each SIP
  instalment** separately, not to the SIP as a whole.
