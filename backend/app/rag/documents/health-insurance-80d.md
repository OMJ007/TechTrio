## Limits

Section 80D of the 1961 Act — **Section 126** under the Income-tax Act, 2025 —
covers health insurance premiums. It sits **outside and on top of** the ₹1.5
lakh 80C limit, and like 80C it is available **only under the old regime**.

| Who is covered | Deduction |
| --- | --- |
| Self, spouse, dependent children (all below 60) | Up to ₹25,000 |
| Self, spouse, children where the eldest insured is 60+ | Up to ₹50,000 |
| Parents below 60 | Additional ₹25,000 |
| Parents aged 60+ | Additional ₹50,000 |

The two blocks stack. A 45-year-old insuring their own family and their
senior-citizen parents can claim up to **₹75,000**. Where the taxpayer is also
60+, the combined ceiling reaches **₹1,00,000**.

Within each block, up to **₹5,000** may be claimed for preventive health
check-ups — and this is the one component that may be paid in cash.

For senior citizens with no health policy at all, actual medical expenditure of
up to ₹50,000 can be claimed in place of a premium.

## Conditions that catch people out

- Premiums must be paid by any mode **other than cash** — net banking, card,
  UPI, cheque. A cash premium is disallowed outright. The ₹5,000 preventive
  check-up is the sole exception.
- The deduction is claimed by whoever **pays** the premium. If your parents pay
  their own policy, you cannot claim it, however dependent they are.
- Premiums for parents-in-law do not qualify. Only your own parents.
- If you pay a multi-year premium in one go, the deduction is spread
  proportionally across the years covered, not claimed entirely upfront.
- The employer's group health cover costs you nothing, so there is no premium
  to claim. Any top-up you buy personally does qualify.

## Why this deduction matters beyond tax

The tax break is the smaller half of the argument. A single hospitalisation in
a metro private hospital routinely runs past ₹5 lakh, and health costs in India
inflate faster than general prices. A family floater of ₹10–15 lakh sum insured
costs far less than most people assume when bought in their thirties, and the
premium rises with entry age.

Employer cover is not a substitute. It ends the day the job does, it rarely
covers parents adequately, and the sum insured is usually thin. Buy a personal
policy alongside it, early, while you are still insurable without exclusions.

## Interaction with the regime choice

80D is one of the deductions that determines whether the old regime is worth
choosing at all. Someone paying ₹22,000 for their own family and ₹45,000 for
senior parents is claiming ₹67,000 here — which, alongside a full 80C and any
HRA, can tip the comparison decisively toward the old regime.

Under the new regime the premium is still worth paying. It just does not reduce
your tax.
