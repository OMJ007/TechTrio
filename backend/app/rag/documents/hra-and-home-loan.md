## House Rent Allowance

HRA is an **exemption**, not a deduction: the exempt portion never enters
taxable income. It is available **only under the old regime**, and only to
salaried people whose salary structure actually contains an HRA component.

The exemption is the **least** of these three:

1. Actual HRA received
2. 50% of basic salary for metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai),
   40% elsewhere
3. Rent paid **minus** 10% of basic salary

Worked example — Bengaluru, basic ₹6,00,000, HRA received ₹3,00,000, rent
₹25,000/month:

- Actual HRA: ₹3,00,000
- 40% of basic (non-metro for HRA purposes): ₹2,40,000
- Rent ₹3,00,000 − 10% of basic ₹60,000 = ₹2,40,000

The least is ₹2,40,000, so that much is exempt and ₹60,000 of the HRA is taxed.

Note that for HRA the metro list is limited to those four cities. Bengaluru,
Hyderabad and Pune take the 40% rate.

**Practical requirements**

- Keep rent receipts and the rent agreement.
- If annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000, your landlord's PAN must be reported to
  your employer.
- Rent paid to a parent is allowed if the arrangement is genuine — they must
  actually own the property, and they must declare the rent as income. Rent
  paid to a spouse is generally challenged.
- If you receive no HRA at all but pay rent, Section 80GG allows a much smaller
  deduction, also old regime only.

## Home loan interest — Section 24(b), now Section 20

Interest on a home loan for a **self-occupied** property is deductible up to
**₹2,00,000** a year. Old regime only.

For a **let-out** property the interest deduction is uncapped, but the overall
house-property loss you may set off against other income is limited to
₹2,00,000 a year; the rest carries forward for up to eight years.

The **principal** repayment is a separate matter — it goes under 80C (Section
123) and shares the ₹1.5 lakh ceiling with everything else there.

Interest paid before possession (the construction period) is not lost. It is
claimed in five equal instalments starting from the year possession is taken,
within the same ₹2,00,000 cap.

## The additional first-time-buyer deductions

Sections 80EE and 80EEA — now **Sections 130 and 131** — gave first-time buyers
an extra interest deduction beyond the ₹2,00,000. Both were tied to loans
sanctioned within specific windows that have since closed. If your loan was
sanctioned inside one of those windows you may still be claiming it through the
loan's life; new borrowers cannot start one. Check the sanction date before
assuming eligibility.

## HRA and home loan together

You can claim both in the same year, and it is legitimate more often than
people think — you work and rent in one city while owning a home in another, or
your owned property is genuinely let out. What does not survive scrutiny is
claiming HRA for rent while living in the very property whose loan interest you
are also claiming as self-occupied.

## The regime question

Both HRA and 24(b) vanish under the new regime. For someone with a ₹2 lakh
interest claim and ₹2.4 lakh of HRA exemption, that is ₹4.4 lakh of relief lost
— usually enough on its own to make the old regime the better choice. Run both
calculations; do not assume the default regime is right for you.
