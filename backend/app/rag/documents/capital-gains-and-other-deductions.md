## Capital gains on equity

**Listed equity shares and equity mutual funds**

- Held 12 months or less: short-term. Taxed at **20%**.
- Held more than 12 months: long-term. Gains up to **₹1,25,000** a year are
  exempt; the excess is taxed at **12.5%**, without indexation.

The ₹1.25 lakh exemption is per financial year, per taxpayer, and does not
carry forward. Unused, it is gone.

**Debt mutual funds** bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your slab rate
regardless of holding period — the indexation benefit and the long-term rate
were both withdrawn for them.

**Property and unlisted assets** held over 24 months are long-term, taxed at
12.5% without indexation. For property acquired before 23 July 2024, taxpayers
may choose between 12.5% without indexation and 20% with it, whichever is
lower.

Capital gains tax rules are the same under both regimes — these are special
rates, not slab income.

## Harvesting the exemption

If you hold equity funds with unrealised long-term gains, selling enough units
each year to realise up to ₹1,25,000 of gain and immediately reinvesting resets
your cost base at no tax cost. Done annually over a long holding period, this
quietly removes a large amount of eventual tax.

Two cautions: transaction costs and the exit load, and the fact that this only
works for gains that are genuinely long-term. It is bookkeeping, not market
timing — you are out of the market for a day or two at most.

## Interest income — 80TTA and 80TTB

**80TTA**: up to ₹10,000 of interest from savings bank accounts, for those
under 60.

**80TTB**: up to ₹50,000 of interest from savings accounts *and* fixed
deposits, for those 60 and above. A senior citizen claims 80TTB, not 80TTA.

Both are old regime only. Both are commonly missed, because the interest is
often small enough that people forget it is taxable at all — it is, at slab
rate, whether or not TDS was deducted.

## Education loan interest — Section 80E (now 129)

The **entire interest** on an education loan is deductible, with no upper
limit, for up to eight years from the year repayment begins. The loan must be
from a bank or approved financial institution, for higher education, for
yourself, your spouse, your children, or a student you are legal guardian to.
Principal repayment does not qualify. Old regime only.

Because it is uncapped, this is one of the largest deductions available to
young professionals servicing a large education loan — and a strong argument
for the old regime in the early repayment years when interest dominates the
EMI.

## Donations — Section 80G (now 133)

Donations to approved funds and institutions are deductible at either 100% or
50% of the amount, sometimes subject to a ceiling of 10% of adjusted gross
total income, depending on the recipient. Cash donations above ₹2,000 do not
qualify. Old regime only.

Check the institution's 80G registration before donating if the deduction
matters to you; registration lapses are common.

## What survives under the new regime

Very little of the above. Capital gains rates are unchanged, employer NPS under
80CCD(2) continues, and the standard deduction of ₹75,000 applies. Everything
else on this page requires the old regime.
