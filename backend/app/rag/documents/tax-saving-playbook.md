## Decide the regime first

Every other decision depends on it. Estimate your deductions for the year —
EPF, insurance premiums, rent, home loan interest, NPS — and compute tax both
ways. If the total relief is under roughly ₹4.5 lakh, the new regime almost
certainly wins and there is no tax reason to lock money into 80C instruments
at all.

Do this in **April**, not March. The declaration you give your employer at the
start of the year sets your monthly TDS; getting it wrong means either a large
refund you waited a year for, or a painful deduction in the final quarter.

## If the old regime wins, work in this order

1. **Count what already counts.** EPF, term insurance premiums, home loan
   principal, children's tuition. Most salaried people have ₹50,000–₹1,00,000
   of 80C filled before they invest anything.
2. **Fill the 80C gap deliberately** — ELSS for a horizon beyond five years,
   PPF for the guaranteed portion. Spread it as a monthly SIP from April rather
   than a lump sum in March.
3. **Health insurance under 80D.** Family cover, and separately for parents.
   Pay by any non-cash mode.
4. **NPS for the extra ₹50,000** under 80CCD(1B), if the money can stay locked
   until 60.
5. **Claim HRA properly** — receipts kept, landlord's PAN reported if annual
   rent crosses ₹1,00,000.
6. **Home loan interest** up to ₹2,00,000 under Section 24(b).
7. **The small ones people forget**: savings interest under 80TTA/80TTB,
   education loan interest under 80E, donations under 80G.

## If the new regime wins

The list is short, which is the point.

- **Ask about corporate NPS.** Employer contribution under 80CCD(2), up to 14%
  of basic, is deductible under the new regime. It is a salary restructuring
  conversation with HR, and it is the largest lever most new-regime taxpayers
  have.
- **Harvest long-term capital gains** up to ₹1,25,000 a year. Regime-independent.
- **Keep investing anyway.** The absence of a deduction changes where the money
  should go, not whether it should be saved. Without the 80C constraint you are
  free to choose an index fund over an ELSS and to keep your money liquid.

## The March rush is the enemy

Most bad financial products in India are sold in the last three weeks of
March — endowment policies, ULIPs, five-year FDs bought at a branch counter
under deadline pressure. The deduction is real; the product is often terrible,
and the commitment lasts years after the tax saving is banked.

Starting in April and investing monthly removes the pressure entirely and
rupee-cost-averages the equity portion.

## Keep the proof

Employers ask for investment proofs around December-January. Keep, through the
year: premium receipts, ELSS/PPF statements, rent receipts and agreement, the
home loan interest certificate from the lender, NPS transaction statement, and
80G receipts. Missing a proof means the TDS is deducted anyway, and you are
back to claiming a refund at filing time.

## A note on priority

Tax saving is a secondary objective. An instrument that is wrong for your
horizon does not become right because it carries a deduction, and ₹46,800 of
tax saved does not compensate for fifteen years in a product yielding 4%.
Choose the investment first, then check whether it happens to qualify.
